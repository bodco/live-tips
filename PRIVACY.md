# Pacenote privacy policy

Updated 17 November 2026. The full text is on the website: **[pacenote.bodco.pm/privacy/](https://pacenote.bodco.pm/privacy/)** ([українською](PRIVACY.uk.md)).

In short:

- **Audio stays on your Mac.** Speech is recognised locally with Whisper; audio is not sent to any AI service. It is written to disk only when you record a call (below).
- **Call recording (0.9.5), off by default.** If you turn it on in Settings > Call recording or choose it for a call, Pacenote saves the call's sound, or the sound and the screen (a whole screen or one window, never Pacenote's own windows), as a file in the project's `video/` folder on your Mac. It is never sent to an AI. It goes to Google Drive only when you upload it, or when you choose to upload every recording: into your own Drive (My Drive > Pacenote > the project), with the `drive.file` permission that lets Pacenote see only the files it made. You can move or share that folder in Drive; Pacenote keeps writing into it. If you turn on Transcript in Drive (0.11.0, off by default), the call's transcript (speaker names, their lines and times, the title, date, project and participants; no tips or notes) goes as a text file into the same folder. Nothing is shown or played to the other participants: telling them is up to you (Settings > General > Recording consent can say or copy a message). Since 0.13.0 the call's files note whether that message was said or copied and when, never its text.
- **Transcripts, voice prints, settings and logs** stay in your project folders, the app's folder and the macOS Keychain.
- **Text leaves the Mac only to services you set up with your own keys or accounts:** Anthropic, through your API key or your Claude account with Claude Code, for tips, preparation, search and the wrap-up; OpenAI, if selected in Settings > AI, through your API key for live tips and after-call jobs (Responses API, `store: false`), or through your ChatGPT account using the official Codex CLI for preparation, tips and after-call work (ephemeral sessions, CLI-managed credentials); Google Gemini, if selected in Settings > AI or turned on as the backup when Claude or ChatGPT is out, through your own API key of a paid-tier project, for preparation, tips and after-call jobs (the classic streaming API, no stored interactions; Google does not use paid-tier content to improve its products, free-tier content can be used, so use a paid-tier key); your Google account through Google's Antigravity CLI, if you choose it for the preparation or after the call (or as the backup with no Gemini key): Google's Antigravity terms let Google use your conversations, the call text included, to improve its research and products and let people at Google review them, Telemetry off does not stop storage, and the terms forbid use through third-party software such as Pacenote, which may lead Google to suspend the account; Pacenote removes agy's local copy of each conversation it made; Notion for the meeting page; HubSpot, if you connect it, for the CRM update you approve after a call; web pages you name in the preparation; one-time model and package downloads (Hugging Face, GitHub, PyPI).
- **HubSpot (0.8.7).** The service key stays in the macOS Keychain. Pacenote reads the company, the deal, contacts and recent activity into a local snapshot next to the brief. It writes a note, deal fields and a task only after you tick them in Update HubSpot and press Apply; the transcript goes into the note only if you turn that on (off by default).
- **Google (0.9.1).** If you connect Google accounts, Pacenote reads their calendars, the Google Docs, Sheets and Slides you link in a call's setup or that are attached to the invitation, and the Google Meet participants and transcript of a call when you ask for them. Another Drive file (a PDF or Word file) is read only when you confirm that one file in Google Picker with Confirm in Google (0.10.0); Pacenote never has access to the rest of your Drive. It writes only a new Google Doc when you export notes there, and the call recordings (and, if you turn it on, transcripts) you upload to Drive. Copies of the documents stay in the project folder on your Mac; their text goes to the AI service of the preparation like your other files. The tokens stay in the macOS Keychain; Remove revokes them. Pacenote's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the [Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), including the Limited Use requirements; it is not used for advertising, not sold and not used to train models.
- **Pacenote account and server (0.11.0; calls since 0.12.0), optional.** Settings > Account can sign you in to the Pacenote server with Google. Signing in gives Pacenote only your Google email, name and picture; the server has no access to your Drive, Calendar or Docs, and this sign-in is separate from the Google accounts you connect for calendars and Drive. The server keeps your account and the list of your signed-in devices (name, app version, last activity). Since 0.12.0, for each project where you turn on Sync to Pacenote server (Settings > Projects > the project's menu; off by default), the app sends after each call, and again when its notes or Meeting Sheet change: the call's title, time, length, type, audience and language, its project, the call notes as the summary, decisions, tasks, risks (since 0.12.2, with Pacenote server 0.4.0) and the Meeting Sheet, plus links to your recording and transcript in Google Drive. A call's page can leave that one call out, or delete that call from the server (it is removed at once and not sent again). Since 0.13.0 it also sends the call's consent record (whether the consent message was said, copied for the meeting chat or off, and when; never the message) and its numbers: how many live tips were shown and rated useful or not, how many times you pressed HELP NOW, your share of the talking and how many Meeting Sheet topics came up (never the text of tips). Participants' names, whether they are from your company and how long each one spoke are not sent yet; this policy will say so before they are. Participants' email addresses are never sent. Audio, video, transcripts, voice prints and the text of live tips never go to the Pacenote server. The server and its backups are in the EU (Hetzner, Finland); the content of your calls is encrypted with a key of your own, kept apart from the database. The sign-in tokens stay in the macOS Keychain. Export everything of yours on the server as a zip, sign out on this Mac or everywhere, or delete your server data, in Settings > Account at any time; deleting all your data: your content key is destroyed at once and the rest is removed within 30 days, also from backups. Without signing in, nothing changes.
- **Organizations (0.17.0, beta).** If you join an organization in Pacenote, your team lead will see the summaries, decisions, tasks, risks and call details of your calls in that organization, and on the Enterprise plan so will its leadership (C-level), as the organization sets it up; admins manage people but do not read call content. If your project is linked to an organization project, the other people on that project will see the decisions, tasks and risks of your calls in it (not the calls, summaries, Meeting Sheets or transcripts), and everyone on the project can read and edit its shared project card, project state and list of sources, with a history of who changed what. Private calls, and calls in your personal workspace, stay private: their decisions, tasks and risks are not shared. To help find shared files, the server may also keep a project's file index: the names, dates and short descriptions (the first 600 characters) of the Drive files listed in the project's sources, with a search vector, written on the Mac of a person who can open them; the files themselves never go to the Pacenote server. Recordings and transcripts in Google Drive are seen by whoever has access to that Drive folder, which you or your organization decide in Google Drive.
- **Company web search before sales calls (0.8.7).** On by default, switch it off in Settings > AI. Only the company's name or domain goes into the search, never anything from your files; the brief says what was searched.
- **Local usage log (0.8.7).** Pacenote keeps a log of which features you use (event names and numbers, never the words of a call, names or titles) in the app's folder, one file a month, 12 months kept. It stays on your Mac; switch it off in Settings > General > Privacy.
- **Help improve Pacenote (product analytics; 0.19.0, on by default).** Unless you turn it off in Settings > General > Privacy, Pacenote sends the author which screens you open, which buttons and features you use, errors as fixed codes (never their text), how long things take, and once a day which features and AI routes are switched on. Each event has a time, a random session id and only values from a fixed list: never the words of a call, the text of tips, notes, names of people, projects, clients or files, titles, emails or what you type. Events are sent every hour to the Pacenote server (pacenote-api.bodco.pm). When you are signed in they are linked to your Pacenote account, your organization and its plan; a hash of your Mac's hardware id tells your Macs apart. Pacenote staff use them to see how the app is used and where it fails; the owner and admins of your organization see usage of the organization and of each member (last active, number of calls and hours, which features are used), never your individual events, screens, errors or anything from your calls. The owner or admins of an organization can turn analytics on for work in that organization: the app then sends it for that organization's calls and screens even if you turned it off for yourself, and Settings > General > Privacy says so. The server keeps no IP address, keeps events 13 months and then only totals without people. When you delete your account, your events lose any link to you and your organization. Show what is sent shows the next batch before it goes. Your data export includes your events. The same hash of your Mac's hardware id goes with signing in, so the server tells one Mac's devices and events together. For now the weekly count of earlier versions also goes next to it, once a week: event names and numbers of whole days, not linked to your account, under the same switch.
- **Otherwise nothing is sent to the author** (the Pacenote account above is the only exception, and only if you sign in). No analytics SDK, no tracking, no crash reporting. Nothing is sent to clients by itself.
- **You are responsible for the people on your calls:** tell them you transcribe, record if you do, and use an AI assistant, and get consent where it is required. Summaries and Meeting Sheets can hold confidential or personal information about them and your clients: send to the Pacenote server only what you and your organization may store there. Pacenote's consent record only notes that a message was said or copied; it does not show that anyone agreed.
